# Offer letter — clauses flagged for a decision

The letter in this folder has **one** change from the version issued: "a batch of 17
trainees" corrected to "a batch of 12 trainees". Nothing else was altered — the items below
are terms for the company to decide, not edits to make quietly.

Ranked by how much trouble each causes if left as it is.

### 1. ₹20,000 is stated as CTC with no break-up
CTC includes PF, ESI and other statutory deductions, so in-hand will be visibly less than
twenty thousand. Every trainee discovers this on the first payday and every one of them
feels misled. This is the most common cause of resentment and early exits in a trainee
batch. Add a one-page annexure showing gross, deductions and in-hand.

### 2. The Gateway has no re-test
Clause 3 is pass-or-exit with no second attempt. Four months of investment discarded
because someone had fever that week. Recommend one re-test within 15 days, at the company's
discretion, for a trainee whose monthly reviews have been satisfactory.

### 3. Clause 3 and clause 13 contradict each other
Clause 3 says a Gateway failure means the offer "shall stand concluded without further
obligation on either side". Clause 13 says termination during probation needs 7 days'
notice or pay in lieu. A Gateway exit happens during probation — so which applies? Pick one
and say so, or have the argument with a distressed 21-year-old in January.

### 4. No leave policy at all
Four offs a month are weekly offs, not leave. The letter is silent on casual or sick leave
during probation. Someone will ask on day three, get an inconsistent answer, and it becomes
a precedent. State it, even if the answer is "no paid leave during Phase 1, offs only".

### 5. No confidentiality clause
The Conflict of Interest declaration in Annexure A is not the same thing. These twelve
handle drawings, contractor rates, contract terms, BOQs and customer data from week one. A
short confidentiality and company-property clause belongs in the letter itself.

### 6. Month 5 appears in both Phase 1 and Phase 2
Clause 3 puts the Gateway in Month 5; clause 4 puts deployment in "Months 5 to 9". Minor,
but it is the kind of thing that gets quoted back at you. Say the Gateway is in the first
week of Month 5 and deployment runs from the balance of Month 5 to Month 9.

### 7. No training bond — and that looks right
Worth naming the reasoning so it is a decision rather than an omission. At ₹20,000 CTC a
bond would be hard to enforce, poor for the employer brand in Gwalior, and unnecessary: the
9-month probation with confirmation at the end already *is* the retention mechanism. What
matters is that confirmation carries a real, visible improvement in terms — otherwise the
mechanism does not work.

### 8. "Offs availed only with Reporting Manager's approval"
Read literally, clause 10 could be taken as making weekly rest discretionary. Almost
certainly not the intent — but whoever handles Shops & Establishments compliance should
read it before it goes out. Flagged, not advised on.

### 9. The Reporting Manager is never named
Clause 11 says it "may change with phase and department allotment", which is true, but a
trainee joining on day one should be handed a name. Give them Rajat's name in the joining
briefing even though the letter stays generic — the first question every one of them has is
who they answer to.
