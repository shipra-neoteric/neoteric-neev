What the offer letter changed

Reading the NEEV offer letter changes the shape of this plan in one important way, and it is worth being blunt about it before anything else.

The 30-day plan was built as a whole programme. It is actually Month 1 of four.

The offer letter commits to Phase 1 training across Months 1 to 4, a Gateway Assessment in Month 5, and department deployment from Months 5 to 9. So September is not the training programme — September is the foundation month of the training programme. Months 2, 3 and 4 exist, are contractually promised, and need designing.

The second change is the tracks. I had invented three tracks. The letter already names the four that matter, because they are the four departments a trainee is actually allotted to at the Gateway: Supervision (Work), Quality, Measurement, Store. Every rotation from here is built on those four and nothing else — a rotation that does not map to a real allotment decision is a rotation that teaches nobody anything useful about where they belong.

Everything already written — the 22 handbook modules, the assessment method, the reporting design — survives intact. What changes is the frame around it, which is now the frame the trainees signed.

01

The nine months, in plain words

You asked me to explain this simply. Here it is with no jargon.

Think of it the way a hospital trains a new doctor. Nobody asks a fresh graduate on day one whether they want to be a surgeon or a physician. They spend time in each ward. By the end, the hospital knows where the person is good and the person knows where they are happy — and then the speciality is decided. NEEV works the same way.

Why the rotation matters more than the training

Anyone can teach a diploma holder what a slump test is. The hard problem is knowing, nine months from now, which of these twelve should be running work on a floor, which should be checking quality, which should be measuring and billing, and which should be running your store. You cannot decide that from an interview or a marksheet. You decide it by watching each person do all four jobs — and the rotation is the only mechanism that produces that evidence.

Put differently: the rotation is not for them. It is for you.

03

Track rotation — how it actually works

Twelve trainees. Four departments. The whole trick is making sure every person sees every department without any department ever having more than three trainees underfoot at once.

Step one: four pods of three

Split the twelve into four small groups of three. Call them Pod 1, 2, 3 and 4. A pod stays together for the whole of Phase 1 — they learn from each other, and one site buddy can genuinely supervise three people.

Pods are formed on 12 September, after the first checkpoint assessment, not on day one. Each pod gets one strong, one middle and one developing trainee. Do not put the three quickest together: you get one pod that races ahead, three that stall, and no peer teaching anywhere.

Step two: the taster, in Month 1

In the last two weeks of September each pod spends three or four days in each of the four departments. Nobody becomes good at anything in four days — that is not the point. The point is that by 30 September every trainee has stood inside all four jobs, and you have four department heads who have each watched all twelve people.

Three months deep in three departments, then one week in the fourth so nobody walks into the Gateway with a blank space. Every department is staffed by exactly one pod every month, which is what makes this affordable in supervision time.

The one rule that makes an immersion different from a visit

In Month 1 a trainee watches the store. In Month 2 the trainee in Store owns the reconciliation of two materials for the month and signs it. In Quality they own the checklists for one floor. In Measurement they own the take-off for one flat type. In Supervision they own one trade on one floor.

Small enough to control, real enough that getting it wrong shows. "Assist the Site Engineer" is not a scope, and it is the default every trainee programme drifts into — which is why most of them produce nothing.

Bharti runs the office side in parallel, not in this chain: Annexure A document collection and verification, printing and issuing handbooks, attendance data to payroll, the training file for each trainee, and the interface to Shubhra in HR. Nothing in the chain above should ever be waiting on a document.

Deepti owns it — so her time has to be protected in writing

Rajat running the daily rhythm is not the same as Deepti owning the outcome, and the second one is the one that quietly evaporates. If Deepti is not formally released from part of her existing load, the weekly review becomes fortnightly, then monthly, then a WhatsApp message. Twelve people over nine months is not a side project.

Practically: Deepti's Saturday review with you is the load-bearing meeting of this entire programme. Put it in the calendar as recurring, and treat a cancelled one as a problem rather than a scheduling detail.

One thing to watch between Rajat and Deepti

The trainees will work out within a fortnight which of the two gives the softer answer, and will start routing requests there. Agree the boundary on day one and say it out loud to the batch: anything about the day goes to Rajat; anything about their assessment, their band, or their future goes to Deepti. Neither overturns the other in front of a trainee.

Built to match the offer letter exactly — 09:00 to 18:00, four offs a month. September 2026 has four Sundays, so taking Sundays as the offs gives exactly the four the letter promises and 26 working days.

Eight early mornings, scheduled in advance

The contractor's muster at 07:30 is the single best lesson in manpower planning and contractor behaviour available anywhere on site, and a 09:00 start means they would never see it. So schedule it: on 2, 5, 8, 14, 17, 21, 24 and 28 September the batch reports at 07:30 and leaves at 16:30. The letter's "site postings may occasionally require adjusted hours" clause covers this, and telling them the eight dates on day one is the difference between a programme feature and a grievance.

Measuring learning speed

You wanted to judge how fast each person learns, not just what they know. Score and speed are different things, and the difference is worth the arithmetic.

Velocity = (Score now − Baseline) ÷ (100 − Baseline) × 100

A trainee who goes 30 → 70 captured 57% of the improvement available to them. One who goes 65 → 75 captured 29%. The second has the better marks and the worse velocity — and over nine months the first one overtakes them. Diploma holders from different branches start at wildly different points, which is exactly why raw marks in September would mislead you.

BandThresholdWhat it triggers
AFastScore ≥ 75 and Velocity ≥ 55Given the harder scope in each immersion. Watch as future department leads.
BOn trackScore ≥ 60Standard immersion scope with daily supervision.
CDevelopingScore ≥ 45Written improvement plan naming the two specific gaps, a named mentor, and a re-check the following month.
DAt riskScore < 45Honest conversation, in writing, with Deepti and Bharti present. Not an exit — the Gateway is the exit point. But they must be told in September that they are on this path, not discover it in January.

Do not let anyone reach the Gateway surprised

The letter makes Month 5 binary: clear it or the programme ends. That is a fair term, but it is only fair if the trainee has been told the truth every month before it. A band-D trainee in September who is first told in January has been failed by the programme, not by themselves — and everyone else in the batch will read it that way too.

07

The offer letter — nine things worth a second look

I corrected the headcount from 17 to 12 and left everything else alone, because these are your terms to set and not mine to quietly change. Ranked by how much trouble each one causes if left as it is.

01
₹20,000 is stated as CTC, with no break-up

CTC includes PF, ESI and other statutory deductions, so in-hand will be visibly less than twenty thousand. Every trainee will discover this on the first payday and every one of them will feel misled — this is the single most common cause of resentment and early exits in a trainee batch. Add a one-page annexure showing gross, deductions and in-hand. It costs you nothing and it buys the whole first month of goodwill.

02
The Gateway has no re-test

Clause 3 is pass-or-exit with no second attempt. Four months of investment discarded because someone had one bad day, or fever, or a family emergency that week. Recommend one re-test within 15 days, at the company's discretion, for a trainee whose monthly reviews have been satisfactory. It costs you almost nothing and removes the most obvious unfairness in the document.

03
Clause 3 and clause 13 contradict each other

Clause 3 says a Gateway failure means the offer "shall stand concluded without further obligation on either side". Clause 13 says termination during probation needs 7 days' notice or pay in lieu. A Gateway exit happens during probation, so which applies? Pick one and say so, otherwise you are having that argument with a distressed 21-year-old in January.

04
No leave policy at all

Four offs a month are weekly offs, not leave. The letter is silent on casual or sick leave during probation. Someone will ask on day three, get an inconsistent answer from whoever they ask, and it will become a precedent. State it, even if the answer is "no paid leave during Phase 1, offs only".

05
No confidentiality clause

There is a Conflict of Interest declaration in Annexure A, which is not the same thing. These twelve will handle drawings, contractor rates, contract terms, BOQs and customer data from week one. A short confidentiality and company-property clause belongs in the letter itself.

06
Month 5 appears in both Phase 1 and Phase 2

Clause 3 puts the Gateway in Month 5 and clause 4 puts department deployment in "Months 5 to 9". Minor, but it is the kind of thing that gets quoted back at you. Say the Gateway is in the first week of Month 5 and deployment runs from the balance of Month 5 to Month 9.

07
No training bond — and I think that is right

Worth naming the reasoning so it is a decision rather than an omission. At ₹20,000 CTC a bond would be hard to enforce, poor for your employer brand in Gwalior, and unnecessary: the 9-month probation with confirmation at the end already is your retention mechanism. What matters is that the confirmation carries a real, visible improvement in terms, or the mechanism does not work.

08
"Offs availed only with Reporting Manager's approval"

Read literally this could be taken as making weekly rest discretionary. It almost certainly is not the intent — but have whoever handles your Shops & Establishments compliance read clause 10 before it goes out. I am flagging it, not advising on it.

09
The Reporting Manager is never named

Clause 11 says it "may change with phase and department allotment", which is true, but a trainee joining on day one should be handed a name. Put Rajat's name in the joining briefing even though the letter stays generic — the first question every one of them has is who they answer to.

What I did change: "a batch of 17 trainees" to "a batch of 12 trainees", and nothing else. The corrected file is attached.
